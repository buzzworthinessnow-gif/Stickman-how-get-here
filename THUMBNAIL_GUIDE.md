# 16:9 YouTube Thumbnail / Cover Image Workflow

For each finished long-form episode, **propose 4–6 distinct 16:9 thumbnail concepts** based on verified story beats and recognizable objects in the actual video. Generate 4–6 candidate images **when the image tool is available and image creation is authorized**; otherwise supply 4–6 ready-to-use detailed prompts and headline options. Do not claim thumbnails were rendered or saved without checking.

## Thumbnail design rules

- **16:9 landscape**, preferably **1280×720 pixels** or a higher-resolution 16:9 source; ensure final export meets the chosen platform's current requirements.
- Reuse the approved white-faced, dark-outline stickman style with lightly dressed era-specific hats, shirts/jackets, pants/shorts, and shoes. Keep a consistent recognizable character while varying facial expressions and poses.
- Give the historical object or invention strong visual emphasis; use rich, accurate illustrated settings and dramatic but legible lighting.
- Choose **one strong curiosity hook** per thumbnail; avoid crowded timelines or too many subjects. Create multiple **individually usable images**, not one six-panel collage, unless the user explicitly asks for a contact sheet.
- Large readable **2–5-word headline**, high contrast, placed away from faces and important mechanisms. Prefer adding accurate headline typography in an editor rather than trusting image-generation lettering; verify spelling and legibility on mobile.
- Use emotional contrast, a surprising verified historical attempt, before/after, invention mistake, big transformation, or mystery. No false danger, invented explosions, fabricated “first” claims, or misleading thumbnails.
- Keep important elements away from edges; test at phone-size preview. Distinguish each candidate with composition, color, character emotion, and headline.
- Generate from the specific episode's actual researched narrative, not generic clickbait. Never imply the video covers something it does not.

## Six sample thumbnail directions for “How Did We Get Here? The Car”

| Candidate | Headline | Visual hook |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | HOW DID CARS BEGIN? | Confused inventor between a horse carriage and an early motorcar |
| 2 | STEAM VS GAS! | Dramatic but accurate split: steam road vehicle and early gasoline car |
| 3 | THE CAR'S BIG GAMBLE | Expressive inventor inspecting an unreliable early mechanism; no fictional catastrophe |
| 4 | ONE IDEA CHANGED ROADS | Detailed early automobile against modern traffic transformation |
| 5 | BEFORE THE HIGHWAY | Dusty historical road evolving into a modern multi-lane road |
| 6 | WHAT CAME FIRST? | Three recognizable power concepts: steam, electric, gasoline |

## Per-thumbnail prompt formula

“Standalone **16:9 landscape YouTube thumbnail** for [episode and fact-based hook]. [One central era-appropriate white-faced expressive 2D stickman, outfit details, pose/emotion] interacting with [specific accurate historical object]. [One strong contrast or mystery object] in [rich illustrated period setting]. [Clear foreground/midground/background], dramatic cinematic light, bold clean silhouette, high contrast, intentional negative space for [2–5-word headline]. Tactile ink-and-paper storybook look, restrained but vivid accents. No photorealistic people, anachronisms, false events, watermarks, tiny unreadable labels, or crowded montage.”

## Delivery and preservation

Record for each candidate: thumbnail ID, headline, verified hook/fact, full image prompt, generated asset reference or filename, and selected/rejected status. Save approved assets in the authorized project or `episodes/<episode-id>/thumbnails/` when supported. A GitHub Markdown prompt list does not mean actual image binaries have been committed. Ask before publishing or changing an existing YouTube thumbnail.
