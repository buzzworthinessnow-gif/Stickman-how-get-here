# Stickman Wardrobe Style Guide

Use this guide with SYSTEM_PROMPT.md for all future episodes.

## Signature character style

Keep the expressive, white round face, bold black outline, slim stickman limbs, oversized friendly eyes, and readable cartoon gestures shown in the approved sample. Add **light, theme-specific clothing** without losing the recognizable stickman silhouette. The visual contrast is intentional: simple cartoon people against richly detailed historical vehicles, machinery, architecture, and scenery.

## Wardrobe by time and role

For every visible character, specify:
- Headwear: type, material, color, or explicitly none
- Top: shirt or blouse, sleeves, collar, material, color
- Optional outer layer: jacket, coat, vest, or overalls
- Bottom: trousers, shorts, or other culturally and historically appropriate garment, including length and color
- Footwear: simple shoes or boots appropriate to the period
- Optional accessory: suspenders, belt, scarf, goggles, apron, or tool belt

Keep outfits restrained: typically one distinctive hat, one or two layers, pants, and shoes. Avoid elaborate costume clutter. Use role, period, geography, season, and setting to select clothing; do not use modern items in early historical scenes.

## Approved automotive pilot example

A friendly, full-body 2D stickman inventor in an early-20th-century workshop: round white face with expressive eyes and open smile; black line-art arms and legs; brown textured flat cap; cream rolled-sleeve collared shirt; warm brown fitted vest; dark suspenders; charcoal-brown cuffed trousers; brown lace-up work boots. Pose: one hand on hip and the other open toward an early motorcar. Rich workshop background with wood, metal, blueprints, gears, warm sunlight, and era-appropriate architecture. Clothing must not turn the figure into a realistic human.

The wardrobe is an illustrative character design, not a claim about what a particular inventor wore.

## Prompt suffix — append to each image prompt

"Character wardrobe lock: [hat/headwear], [shirt and sleeve style], [jacket or vest], [belt/suspenders], [pants or shorts], [footwear], [accessory], in [colors and materials]. Keep the white circular cartoon face, expressive features, dark stickman outlines, slender line limbs, and simple silhouette. All visible people remain stylized 2D stickmen with modest era-appropriate clothing. Preserve the same clothing cut, colors, accessories, and footwear in connected shots. No realistic human anatomy, no random wardrobe changes, no anachronistic clothing."

## Motion continuity

Hats remain attached to heads unless removal is scripted. Sleeves and trouser legs move naturally during walking, working, and gestures. No garments appear, disappear, or change colors between frames. Keep character identity and silhouette stable across cuts.

## Suggested wardrobe examples

| Setting | Example character outfit |
|---|---|
| Early automotive workshop | Flat cap, rolled-sleeve cream shirt, brown vest, cuffed trousers, leather boots |
| Industrial assembly line | Work cap, blue-gray cotton shirt, dark overalls, work boots |
| Victorian street | Bowler hat, simple coat, waistcoat, trousers, lace-up shoes |
| Early aviation | Leather flight cap, goggles, short flight jacket, trousers, boots |
| Modern technology | Casual jacket, plain T-shirt, jeans, sneakers |

Adapt these examples to the actual historical date, place, and occupation. The clothing design should complement, not obscure, the story action.
